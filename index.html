Part 1 · Conceptual
Newton's Laws of Motion
Ch. 4 & 5
💡
Exam focus: Expect conceptual MCQs on which law applies, what "equilibrium" means, and whether forces cancel. Problem-solving: FBD → axis → equations.
1st Law — Inertia

No net force = no change in motion. An object stays at rest or moves in a straight line at constant speed. Force is needed to change velocity, not to maintain it.

2nd Law — F = ma

Net force causes acceleration in the same direction. Treat each axis separately: ΣFx = max and ΣFy = may. If no acceleration on one axis → that ΣF = 0.

3rd Law — Action-Reaction

Forces come in equal-and-opposite pairs on different objects. They do NOT cancel each other in an FBD because they act on different bodies.

Mass vs. Weight ⚠️
  • Mass (m): kg, scalar, same everywhere
  • Weight (w): N, vector, w = mg
  • If given weight: m = w/g before using F = ma
  • g = 9.80 m/s² on Earth
Equilibrium (1st Law Applied)
  • At rest OR constant velocity → a = 0
  • ΣFx = 0 AND ΣFy = 0
  • "Constant velocity" on incline → net force = 0
  • Normal force on incline = mg·cos(θ)
Friction
  • Static: fs ≤ μs·n, prevents motion, variable
  • Kinetic: fk = μk·n, constant during sliding
  • μs > μk always (harder to start than keep moving)
  • Find n from ΣFy = 0 first, then compute fk
Apparent Weight (Elevator)
  • Accelerating up → n = m(g + a) → heavier
  • Accelerating down → n = m(g − a) → lighter
  • Free fall → n = 0 → weightless
  • Constant v → n = mg → normal
Circular Motion
  • Constant speed, changing direction = acceleration
  • a_rad = v²/R, always toward center
  • "Centripetal force" = net radial force, NOT a new force
  • Car on flat curve: fs = mv²/R → vmax = √(μs·g·R)

Part 1 & 2
Work and Kinetic Energy
Ch. 6
💡
Exam focus: Dot product definition of work, the Work-Energy Theorem, and how to handle work by varying forces (area under F-x graph / integration concept).
Work (W)

Work = Force × displacement in the direction of force. W = F·d·cos(θ). θ is the angle between force and displacement. Perpendicular force does zero work. Work is a scalar (can be negative).

Dot Product
  • W = F⃗ · d⃗ = Fx·dx + Fy·dy
  • W = |F||d|cos(θ)
  • θ = 90° → W = 0 (normal force, centripetal)
  • θ = 180° → W negative (friction)
  • θ = 0° → W = Fd (max positive)
Kinetic Energy & Work-Energy Theorem
  • K = ½mv²
  • W_total = ΔK = K₂ − K₁
  • Net work done on object = change in KE
  • If W_total = 0 → speed is constant
  • Units: Joules (J = N·m = kg·m²/s²)
Varying Forces
  • W = area under the F vs. x graph
  • Mathematically: W = ∫F·dx
  • Constant force: W = F·d (rectangle)
  • Spring force (F = kx): W = ½kx² (triangle)
  • Power: P = W/t = F·v (Watts)

Part 1 & 2
Potential Energy & Conservation of Energy
Ch. 7
⚡
Exam focus: Setting up energy equations, identifying when energy is conserved, finding friction force from energy deficit. Mass often cancels — confirm this when it does.
Gravitational PE

U_grav = mgy. The reference y = 0 is your choice — pick whatever makes the math simplest. Only the change in height matters, not the path taken. PE is shared by the object + Earth.

Conservation of Energy

When only gravity acts (no friction): K₁ + U₁ = K₂ + U₂. Total mechanical energy E = K + U is constant. This means what you gain in height you lose in speed, and vice versa.

With Friction (W_other)
  • K₁ + U₁ + W_other = K₂ + U₂
  • Friction work: W_fric = −f·d (always negative)
  • Energy "lost" = f·d → solve for f
  • Positive W_other = engine/applied force adds energy
Key Shortcuts
  • Ball thrown up: y_max = v₁²/(2g)
  • Frictionless ramp of height h: v_bottom = √(2gh)
  • Both are mass-independent (m cancels)
  • At max height: v = 0, so K₂ = 0
  • At bottom of ramp: U₂ = 0 if y₂ = 0

Part 1 & 2
Fluid Mechanics
Ch. 12
🌊
Exam focus: Density/SG calculations, pressure at depth, hydraulic lift (Pascal), finding buoyant force and apparent weight (Archimedes), continuity, and Bernoulli.
Density, SG, Spec. Weight
  • ρ = m/V (kg/m³), ρ_water = 1000 kg/m³
  • SG = ρ/ρ_water (dimensionless)
  • SG > 1 → sinks; SG < 1 → floats
  • γ = ρg = W/V (N/m³), specific weight
  • γ_water ≈ 9810 N/m³
Pressure & Depth
  • p = F/A (Pa = N/m²)
  • Pressure increases with depth: p = p₀ + ρgh
  • Container shape does NOT matter
  • p_absolute = p_gauge + p_atm
  • p_atm ≈ 1.013 × 10⁵ Pa
Pascal's Law

Pressure applied to enclosed fluid transmits equally everywhere. Hydraulic advantage: F₁/A₁ = F₂/A₂. Energy is conserved: small piston × large distance = large piston × small distance.

Archimedes's Principle
  • B = ρ_fluid · V_submerged · g
  • B = weight of fluid displaced
  • Float condition: B = W_object → V_sub/V_total = ρ_obj/ρ_fluid
  • Apparent weight: W_app = W − B
  • For submerged object: T + B = mg → T = mg − B
Continuity Equation

Incompressible fluid: volume flow rate Q = Av is constant throughout the pipe. A₁v₁ = A₂v₂. If area halves → speed doubles. Thumb on hose → area ↓ → speed ↑.

Bernoulli's Equation

Energy conservation in flowing fluids. Faster flow = lower pressure. p + ρgy + ½ρv² = const. Torricelli (tank leak): both surfaces at atm pressure → v_leak = √(2gh). Same as free fall!

Part 1 & 2
Introduction to Thermodynamics
Thermo I & II
🔥
Exam focus: Temperature conversions (Kelvin required for radiation), specific heat, calorimetry balance equation, latent heat phases, and identifying the 3 modes of heat transfer.
Temperature Scales
  • TK = TC + 273.15 (always Kelvin in equations)
  • TF = (9/5)TC + 32
  • TC = (5/9)(TF − 32)
  • ΔTC = ΔTK (same step size!)
  • ΔTF = (9/5)·ΔTC (do NOT add 32 for ΔT)
  • Absolute zero: 0 K = −273.15°C
Zeroth Law & Heat
  • Zeroth Law: thermal equilibrium is transitive → makes thermometers work
  • Heat (Q): energy transferred due to temp difference
  • Flows hot → cold only
  • Objects contain internal energy, NOT "heat"
  • Units: Joules (J), 1 cal = 4.186 J
Specific Heat (Q = mcΔT)
  • c = specific heat capacity (J/kg·K)
  • Q positive → object gains heat (temp rises)
  • Q negative → object loses heat (temp drops)
  • c_water = 4186 J/kg·K (high → great coolant)
  • c_ice = 2090 J/kg·K, c_steam = 2020 J/kg·K
  • c_copper ≈ 390, c_iron ≈ 500, c_Al ≈ 910 J/kg·K
Calorimetry
  • Insulated system: ΣQ = 0
  • m₁c₁(Tf−Ti1) + m₂c₂(Tf−Ti2) = 0
  • Always write ΔT as (Tf − Ti), not |ΔT|
  • Math auto-assigns negative sign to hot object
  • Solve the linear equation for Tf
Latent Heat & Phase Changes
  • During phase change: temperature stays constant!
  • Q = ±mL (+ melting/boiling, − freezing/condensing)
  • Lf (fusion): solid ↔ liquid (water: 3.33×10⁵ J/kg)
  • Lv (vaporization): liquid ↔ gas (water: 2.26×10⁶ J/kg)
  • Full heating: do Q = mcΔT and Q = mL in stages
Heat Transfer — 3 Modes
  • Conduction: P = kA(Th−Tc)/L, needs physical contact
  • Convection: P = hAΔT, fluid movement carries heat
  • Radiation: P = σAeT⁴, no medium needed, must use Kelvin!
  • Net radiation: Pnet = σAe(T⁴ − Ts⁴)
  • σ = 5.67×10⁻⁸ W/m²·K⁴

🚨
Most common mistakes in Thermo: (1) Using Celsius in Stefan-Boltzmann — ALWAYS convert to Kelvin. (2) Adding 32 when converting a temperature difference — don't! Only multiply by 9/5. (3) Forgetting the latent heat stage in full heating problems.
📌 Exam-Day Power Tips
FBD
Always draw a Free-Body Diagram first. Every mechanics problem. No exceptions. It prevents 90% of sign errors and wrong force setups.
F=ma
If given weight in Newtons, find mass first: m = w/g. Then use F = ma. This is the #1 source of point loss on dynamics problems.
Energy
"Constant velocity" on an incline or any surface means net force = 0, which means you can find the friction coefficient directly from ΣF = 0.
Fluids
Buoyancy: find volume from density first. V = m/ρ_object. Then B = ρ_fluid × V × g. Then apparent weight = true weight − B.
Thermo
Phase change problems: do them in stages. Each temperature segment or phase change is a separate Q = mcΔT or Q = mL. Add all Q values at the end.
MCQ
On conceptual questions, eliminate wrong answers: "centripetal force is a type of force" (wrong), "more force keeps things moving" (wrong), "heat is stored in objects" (wrong).
